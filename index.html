TRACK B SCIENTIFIC PIPELINE

Why Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)?

During catastrophic flood events, severe cloud cover, dense precipitation, and nocturnal timing prevent optical sensors (such as Sentinel-2 or Landsat) from viewing the ground. Sentinel-1 operates an active C-band Synthetic Aperture Radar (5.405 GHz, ~5.6 cm wavelength) that illuminates the Earth with microwave pulses regardless of solar illumination or weather.

1. Specular Reflection

Smooth water surfaces act as specular reflectors, bouncing the radar microwave energy away from the sensor. Water appears as distinct, low-backscatter black signatures (< -20 dB).

2. Double-Bounce Effect

When floodwaters enter built environments, corner reflectors between vertical building walls and horizontal water planes create intense radar double-bounce reflections.

3. Log-Ratio Otsu Segmentation

By performing radiometric calibration, Lee speckle filtering, and log-ratio change detection against pre-event baselines, flood polygons are extracted with >94% F1-score accuracy.
